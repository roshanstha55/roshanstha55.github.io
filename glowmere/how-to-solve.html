How to solve Glowmere puzzles

Every Glowmere board can be solved by logic alone — no guessing, no maths. This guide starts with the rules and builds up to the techniques that crack the 9×9 boards.

The three rules

A board of N×N tiles is split into N colour patches. Place N lights so that:

  1. Every colour patch has exactly one light.
  2. Every row and every column has exactly one light.
  3. Lights never touch — not side by side, and not corner to corner.

In the game, a single tap marks a tile with ✕ (“no light here”) and a double tap places a light. Marks are free notes: use them a lot. Every level has exactly one solution.

First steps

1. The last free tile

When a row, a column or a colour patch has only one tile that isn’t crossed out, its light must go there. This is the move you will make most often, so keep looking for it after every other step.

2. Clear around every light

As soon as a light is placed, cross out everything it rules out: the rest of its row, the rest of its column, the rest of its patch, and the eight tiles touching it. Doing this straight away keeps the board honest and often reveals a “last free tile”.

Intermediate techniques

3. A patch inside one line

If every tile of a patch lies in the same row (or column), that patch’s light is in that line — and because a line holds only one light, every other tile of the line is out. Small, flat patches are the usual place to spot this.

4. Tiles that touch every option

Look at a small patch and ask: which tiles touch all of its possible spots? Those tiles are out, whichever spot wins. Two-cell and three-cell patches give the biggest gains.

5. A line inside one patch

The reverse of technique 3: if a row’s (or column’s) only free tiles all belong to one patch, then that patch’s light is in this line. Cross out the patch’s tiles everywhere else.

Advanced techniques

6. Count the patches (k patches in k lines)

If two patches fit entirely inside the same two rows, those two rows’ lights belong to those two patches. Everything else in the two rows is out. The same works with three patches in three rows, or with columns. Bigger boards (8×8 and 9×9) are often unlocked by this count.

7. A short “what if”

When nothing else moves, pick a tile and imagine a light there. Follow technique 2: if it would leave any patch, row or column with no free tile, the tile is wrong — cross it out. Keep these trials short (one or two steps); a long chain usually means a simpler move was missed.

Tips that save time

  • Start with the smallest patch — it has the fewest options. Glowmere places its light for you at the start of every level.
  • Cross out generously. A crossed tile is a fact you never have to work out again.
  • Stuck? Scan each row, then each column, then each patch for a last free tile before trying anything clever.
  • In the game, a Spark shows the next logical step and explains why — it never just guesses.
