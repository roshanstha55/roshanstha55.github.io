Privacy Policy

App: NFC Kit: Tag Reader & Writer (com.vexelinteractive.nfckit) · Developer: Vexel Interactive, Australia · Last updated: 20 November 2026 · Applies to version 1.0.2 and later

In short

  • Everything you create stays on your phone: your tags, saved tags, history, check-ins, places and parking spot are never sent anywhere.
  • There is no account, no sign-up, no ads and no advertising ID.
  • Optional: if you allow it, NFC Kit sends anonymous usage stats and crash reports to Google Firebase, so we can see which features are used and fix what breaks. You're asked once, and you can change your answer any time in Settings › Share usage stats.
  • Every feature works the same whether you allow it or not, and works offline.

What stays on your phone

  • NFC tag data: when you read or write a tag, its contents (a link, contact card, Wi-Fi details, text, a task or a check-in) are handled on your phone so they can be shown to you or written to the tag.
  • My Tags and history: tags you save and a history of recent reads, writes and task runs are stored in the app's private storage. You can delete them in My Tags at any time.
  • Check-ins and reminders: the times you tap a check-in tag, your streaks and any reminder time are stored on your phone. Check-in tags themselves only hold a name and an ID, never your taps.
  • Tap again to undo: for undo tasks, the setting the first tap changed (for example the flashlight) is kept on your phone until the second tap puts it back.
  • Places: Home and Work addresses you type in Settings › Places stay on your phone and are never written to tags.
  • Android backup: if you have Android's backup turned on, Android may include NFC Kit's data in your own Google account backup and restore it on a new phone. That backup is handled by Android and Google under your account; the developer can't see it. You can turn it off in Android settings.

Usage stats and crash reports (optional)

The first time you open NFC Kit (or the first time after updating to 1.0.2), it asks whether you want to share anonymous usage stats and crash reports. Nothing is sent unless you tap Allow. If you allow it, NFC Kit uses Google's Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics to send:

  • Feature use: which screens you open; which kinds of tag you read or write (for example "Wi-Fi" or "Task"); which task steps, ideas, conditions and undo you use, as fixed labels and counts; which tools you use; when a write fails and the error type; when you reach a limit of this version; and whether you allowed or turned off stats.
  • Crash reports: what the app was doing when it crashed (the code location), plus device model, Android version and app version.
  • Technical details Firebase adds: a random app-instance ID created by Firebase (not linked to your name or Google account), approximate country or region (worked out by Google from your connection, not from GPS), language, and app install source.

Never sent: what's on your tags or what you type (links, text, phone numbers, email addresses, Wi-Fi names or passwords, contact details, addresses, task names, check-in names), your saved tags or history, your location or parking spot, and your advertising ID. NFC Kit turns off the advertising ID and every ad-related signal in Firebase.

Google processes this data for us as a service provider, under the Google Privacy Policy and the Firebase privacy information. We use it only to understand how the app is used and to fix problems. We don't sell it, share it for advertising, or combine it with other data about you. Usage stats are kept for up to 14 months and crash reports for up to 90 days.

To stop: turn off Settings › Share usage stats. Collection stops at once and unsent crash reports are deleted. Uninstalling the app also resets the app-instance ID.

Location

Location is used only by the optional "Remember where I parked" step. NFC Kit asks for permission when you add that step, and reads your location only at the moment you tap that parking tag. The spot is saved on your phone only. There is no background location access, and the location is never sent anywhere, including in usage stats. Opening directions hands the address to your maps app.

Other phone features

  • Notifications: asked for only when you first switch on a check-in reminder, and used only for that reminder.
  • Alarms, calendar and other apps: task steps such as "Set an alarm", "Add a calendar event", "Open an app" or "Open a link" hand the request to the app you choose on your phone. NFC Kit cannot read your alarms, calendar or other apps' data. That app's own privacy policy applies.
  • Installed apps: when you pick an app for a tag, NFC Kit lists the launchable apps on your phone. The list is shown on screen only and is never stored or shared.
  • Clipboard: used only when you tap Copy or a task includes a "Copy text" step.
  • Rating: NFC Kit may ask Google Play to show its rating card. Whether you rate, and what you write, is handled by Google Play.

Deleting your data

There is no account and no NFC Kit server: everything you create is stored only on your phone. To remove it, delete items in the app, clear the app's storage in Android settings, or uninstall the app. Data written to a physical NFC tag stays on that tag until you erase it (Tools › Erase tag).

Usage stats and crash reports aren't linked to your name or account, so we can't look them up by person. Turning off Settings › Share usage stats stops collection; if you'd like us to try to delete stats already sent, email us and we'll explain what Firebase lets us remove.

Children

NFC Kit is a general utility and is not directed at children. It doesn't knowingly collect personal data from anyone, and usage stats are only sent if the person using the phone allows it.

Changes

If this policy changes, for example in a later version with new features, the updated version will be published at this address with a new date, and the change will be described in the app's release notes.
